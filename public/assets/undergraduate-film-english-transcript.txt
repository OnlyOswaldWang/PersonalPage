If university were a game, I didn't begin with a perfect plan. I had to discover my own way forward.

That changed in my first-year C programming class. Writing code felt like building with blocks: a few simple pieces could become something that actually worked. I wanted to keep building.

Finishing top of my cohort in that class gave me confidence. When the chance came to change my degree, I chose to follow my interest in computing.

A university development programme gave me guidance. Like finding a walkthrough in a game, it helped me see what might come next.

But progress came from putting that guidance into practice, one problem at a time.

Mathematical modelling tested that resolve. When an approach failed, we checked our assumptions and tried again. Failure wasn't game over. It was a reason to rethink.

Those long sessions taught me patience: to stay with a difficult problem, and keep working towards an answer.

The awards brought confidence. Each felt like another star collected, a small sign that the effort was paying off.

Missing a scholarship reminded me that confidence also needs careful preparation.

Working with the science society taught me to organise a team and learn from others.

Then came the next step. I chose postgraduate study in robotics at the University of Manchester.

The eight stars marked my progress. Behind each one were decisions, setbacks, and another attempt.

My teammates and tutors helped me find my way, offering ideas, encouragement, and guidance when I needed it.

There was no ready-made route through university. I found mine by trying, learning, and choosing to keep going.

I started without a clear direction. Now I'm ready for the next chapter.
