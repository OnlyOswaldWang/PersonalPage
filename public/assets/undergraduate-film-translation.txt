If university were a game, I didn't begin with a head start.

At school, I hadn't had a clear goal.

But a first-year class in C programming felt like finding my main quest.

Code felt less like homework and more like building blocks.

With the right logic, I could bring things to life.

I came top in that class, and knew I'd found something that suited me.

With a clearer goal, the chance to change degree gave me a fresh choice.

Then I found the university's talent programme.

Like stepping into Minecraft,

I finally had a guide of my own.

But once I started, I realised the guide wasn't just on paper: I had to learn it, page by page.

Mathematical modelling was a tough level.

Once, we worked right up to Chinese New Year's Eve.

If the result was wrong, we'd try again.

Failure wasn't game over. It just meant the guide wasn't finished.

That period brought setbacks, but also revealed my strongest asset:

patience.

So I decided to see it through.

Along the way, I earned these awards.

One star, then another. Each success made me more confident.

But overconfidence brought a setback: I missed the National Scholarship by one place.

Leading the Physical and Chemical Club taught me to build a team,

and develop new sides of myself.

Study at home or abroad? Which map next? I wasn't sure.

I chose to continue studying robotics at the University of Manchester.

Those eight stars came from giving every level my best.

A guide can point the way, but you still have to find your own path.

My teammates helped me along the way, and my tutors guided me through the early stages.

University has no single right answer, only the route you discover for yourself.

I didn't have a perfect start. But I believe

every unseen effort will shine through perseverance.

Even an ordinary beginning can lead somewhere extraordinary.

One level at a time, towards the light.
